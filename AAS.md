# Anti-AI Slop — Website Development Guidelines

When designing and developing this website, follow these principles strictly. The goal is to create something that feels thoughtfully designed by an experienced human designer, not generated from a generic AI website template.

## 1. Design with intention
- Every element should have a clear purpose.
- Avoid generic layouts that look like every other SaaS landing page.
- Don't add elements just to fill empty space.
- Prioritise visual identity and originality.
- Establish a consistent design language throughout the website.
- Make design decisions based on the actual project, not generic trends.

## 2. Avoid generic AI design
- No unnecessary gradients.
- No random floating decorative shapes.
- No excessive rounded cards.
- No unnecessary glassmorphism.
- No repetitive card-based layouts.
- No oversized headings purely for visual impact.
- No random glowing effects or coloured shadows.
- No unnecessary icons beside every heading.
- No generic AI-generated illustrations.
- No meaningless statistics or fake testimonials.

## 3. Layout and spacing
- Use whitespace intentionally.
- Keep layouts balanced and visually structured.
- Avoid cramming too many elements into one section.
- Don't make every section follow the exact same layout.
- Use alignment and spacing to establish hierarchy.
- Keep content widths reasonable.
- Ensure the website looks intentional at different screen sizes.

## 4. Colour and visual identity
- Establish a cohesive colour palette.
- Avoid using too many accent colours.
- Don't rely on gradients to make the design interesting.
- Use contrast to establish hierarchy.
- Make backgrounds, borders and surfaces complement one another.
- Avoid excessive use of pure black and pure white unless appropriate to the design.

## 5. Components and UI
- Components should feel consistent without becoming repetitive.
- Avoid turning every piece of content into a card.
- Use borders and separators where they actually help.
- Keep buttons simple and purposeful.
- Avoid unnecessary animations and transitions.
- Make interactive elements feel responsive and deliberate.
- Don't overcomplicate simple interfaces.

## 6. Animations and interactions
- Animations should serve a purpose.
- Keep transitions subtle and smooth.
- Avoid excessive bouncing, scaling and spinning.
- Don't animate every element when the page loads.
- Use hover effects to communicate interactivity.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Prioritise performance over unnecessary visual effects.

## 7. Content and copywriting
- Avoid generic marketing language.
- Don't use meaningless phrases like "Revolutionising the future" or "Experience the next generation".
- Keep copy natural, concise and relevant.
- Don't invent statistics, reviews or company information.
- Avoid repetitive headings and descriptions.
- Write content that sounds like an actual person.

## 8. Responsive design
- Don't treat mobile responsiveness as an afterthought.
- Design layouts specifically for smaller screens.
- Avoid simply shrinking desktop layouts.
- Ensure navigation works properly on mobile.
- Prevent horizontal overflow.
- Maintain appropriate spacing and readable content.

## 9. Code quality
- Keep the code clean, organised and maintainable.
- Avoid unnecessary dependencies.
- Don't introduce complicated solutions for simple problems.
- Reuse components where appropriate.
- Avoid excessive abstraction.
- Remove unused code and redundant styles.
- Ensure all buttons, links and interactive elements actually work.

## 10. Final quality check

Before considering the website finished, ask:

- Does this look like a generic AI-generated website?
- Are there unnecessary visual elements?
- Does the design have its own identity?
- Is every section necessary?
- Are the layouts becoming repetitive?
- Do animations actually improve the experience?
- Does everything work on mobile?
- Is the website visually consistent?
- Would this look believable as a professionally designed independent project?

**Final rule: Less, but better.**

Don't confuse complexity with quality. A simple, well-executed design is significantly better than an overdesigned website filled with unnecessary effects and components.

Prioritise originality, usability, consistency and attention to detail. The finished website should feel like someone genuinely cared about designing it.
